## Positioning Credit Union Member Engagement as a Managed Solution

## DOCUMENT STRUCTURE (30 Pages, Expandable to Full Playbook)

---

### SECTION 1: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (Pages 1-3)

1.1 The Problem
- Credit unions are losing younger members (18-35) to neobanks and fintech apps
- Member engagement has become transactional rather than relational
- Credit unions lack the technology teams to build and maintain digital community platforms
- Regulatory requirements make adopting consumer social tools (Discord, Slack, WhatsApp) a compliance risk

1.2 The GRYD Solution
- A fully managed member engagement platform delivered as a service
- White-labeled to each credit union's brand identity
- Compliance-ready content moderation, privacy controls, and data governance built in
- Ongoing strategic support, not just software access

1.3 The Business Model
- GRYD is not a software vendor. GRYD is a member engagement partner.
- Revenue model: per-institution monthly retainer (not per-seat SaaS)
- Includes onboarding, configuration, training, ongoing optimization, and platform access
- The technology is the delivery mechanism. The service is the product.

---

### SECTION 2: MARKET CONTEXT (Pages 4-8)

2.1 The Credit Union Landscape
- 5,000+ credit unions in the US alone
- Combined membership exceeding 130 million
- Average credit union lacks dedicated digital product teams
- Growing pressure from regulators and members for digital-first experiences

2.2 The Engagement Gap
- Member interaction limited to transactions (deposits, loans, payments)
- No persistent digital relationship outside of mobile banking apps
- Community events and financial literacy programs have low attendance and no digital footprint
- Younger members see no reason to stay when neobanks offer better UX

2.3 Why Existing Tools Fail Credit Unions
- Consumer platforms (Facebook Groups, WhatsApp) create compliance and data ownership risks
- Enterprise tools (Slack, Teams) are designed for internal teams, not member communities
- Custom development is prohibitively expensive for most credit unions
- No existing solution combines community, engagement, compliance, and analytics in one managed offering

2.4 The Sequoia Thesis Applied
- Reference: Software companies that present as service companies will dominate
- The defensibility is not in the code. It is in the institutional relationship, the data layer, and the managed delivery.
- Distribution advantage: selling to existing institutions eliminates traditional customer acquisition costs

### SECTION 3: THE GRYD SOLUTION ARCHITECTURE (Pages 9-14)

3.1 Platform Capabilities (What the CU Gets)
- Branded mobile app (iOS and Android) and web dashboard
- Community channels (text, announcements, voice)
- Direct messaging between members and between members and CU staff
- Event management and announcements
- Stakeholder and vendor marketplace (future)
- Financial literacy content distribution
- Gamification and engagement scoring (leaderboards, badges, rewards)

3.2 Administration Layer (What the CU Admin Controls)
- Member management, roles, and permissions
- Content moderation with automated filtering
- Privacy and visibility controls
- Notification management
- Sub-community (subgrid) creation and governance
- Analytics dashboard (engagement metrics, active members, content performance)

3.3 The Managed Service Layer (What Makes This a Solution, Not Software)
- Dedicated onboarding: GRYD team configures the platform for each CU's specific needs
- Training: Admin training sessions, member adoption playbooks
- Monthly engagement reports with actionable recommendations
- Ongoing platform optimization based on usage data
- Compliance monitoring and updates as regulations evolve
- Priority support with guaranteed response times

3.4 The Technology Blackbox
- Credit unions do not need to understand or manage the underlying technology
- Updates, security patches, and new features are delivered seamlessly
- The platform scales automatically. No infrastructure decisions required by the CU.
- Data is isolated per institution. Each CU's data is fully segregated and owned by them.

### SECTION 4: DEFENSIBILITY AND COMPETITIVE MOAT (Pages 15-19)

4.1 Why This Is Hard to Replicate
- Layer 1: Institutional Relationships
  - Each CU deployment creates a switching cost. Members build history, connections, and habits.
  - Migrating away means losing all community data, engagement history, and member networks.
  - The longer a CU uses GRYD, the deeper the lock-in becomes.

- Layer 2: Data and Insights
  - Aggregate (anonymized) engagement data across CUs creates a unique dataset
  - AI-powered insights: "CUs in your asset range see 40% higher engagement when they post financial tips on Tuesdays"
  - No competitor can build this dataset without the same institutional access
  - This is the long-term moat: the intelligence layer that improves with every deployment

- Layer 3: Managed Service Wrapper
  - A competitor can clone the app. They cannot clone the onboarding process, the CU-specific configurations, the relationship with the admin team, or the ongoing optimization loop.
  - Service businesses have higher margins and lower churn than pure software businesses.

- Layer 4: Compliance as a Feature
  - Content moderation, privacy controls, and data governance are not features. They are requirements.
  - GRYD builds and maintains compliance so the CU does not have to.
  - Regulatory changes become a moat: each update strengthens the dependency.

4.2 Positioning Against Alternatives
- vs. Building In-House: 10x the cost, 10x the timeline, ongoing maintenance burden
- vs. Consumer Platforms: Compliance risk, no data ownership, no customization
- vs. Other SaaS Tools: GRYD is not a tool. It is a managed engagement partner.

4.3 The Network Effect
- As more CUs adopt GRYD, cross-institution features become possible (with consent)
- Shared vendor/stakeholder marketplace across CU communities
- Benchmarking: "Your engagement is in the top 20% of CUs your size"
- Each new CU makes the platform more valuable for every existing CU

### SECTION 5: BUSINESS MODEL AND PRICING STRATEGY (Pages 20-23)

5.1 Revenue Model
- Monthly retainer per institution (tiered by asset size or member count)
- Includes: platform access, onboarding, training, monthly optimization, support
- No per-seat pricing. This removes friction and aligns incentives with adoption.
- Optional add-ons: marketplace module, advanced analytics, custom integrations

5.2 Pricing Philosophy
- Price on value delivered, not features consumed
- A CU with 50,000 members paying $3,000/month is $0.06 per member per month
- Compare to: cost of losing one member to a neobank (estimated $200+ lifetime value)
- The ROI case writes itself: retain 15 extra members per month and the platform pays for itself

5.3 Expansion Revenue
- Upsell path: basic community to full engagement suite to marketplace to AI insights
- Land with one CU in a league or network, expand to peers through referral
- Annual contracts with auto-renewal create predictable recurring revenue

5.4 Unit Economics Target
- Gross margin target: 80%+ (managed service costs are largely fixed per cohort)
- Payback period: under 6 months per CU deployment
- Net revenue retention target: 120%+ (expansion within existing accounts)

### SECTION 6: GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY (Pages 24-27)

6.1 Distribution Advantage
- Selling to institutions, not individuals
- No social media ad spend. No viral growth hacking. No consumer marketing.
- Sales cycle: direct outreach to CU leadership, conference presence, league partnerships
- One decision-maker signs, 50,000 members get access overnight

6.2 Sales Motion
- Phase 1: Direct outreach to mid-size CUs (10,000-100,000 members)
  - These are large enough to need engagement tools but small enough to lack in-house tech
  - Target: CU marketing directors, member experience leads, CEOs
- Phase 2: Credit union league partnerships
  - Leagues represent dozens or hundreds of CUs
  - One partnership can unlock an entire regional market
- Phase 3: Conference circuit
  - CUNA, NAFCU, state-level CU conferences
  - Live demos, case studies from early adopters

6.3 Proof Points Needed
- 3-5 pilot CUs with measurable engagement lift
- Case study: "XYZ Credit Union increased member engagement by X% in 90 days"
- Testimonial from a CU CEO or board member
- Before/after metrics: member app usage, event attendance, retention rates

6.4 Partnerships
- Integration partnerships with core banking providers (Symitar, Corelation, etc.)
- Reseller partnerships with CU service organizations (CUSOs)
- Technology partnerships for adjacent features (financial literacy content providers)

### SECTION 7: VISION AND ROADMAP (Pages 28-30)

7.1 Phase 1: Foundation (Current)
- Core community platform with channels, messaging, events
- CU Admin dashboard with full management controls
- Content moderation and privacy controls
- Mobile app (iOS, Android) and web access

7.2 Phase 2: Marketplace and Commerce
- Stakeholder/vendor marketplace within CU communities
- Stripe Connect for card payments, crypto wallet integration for alternative payments
- Revenue share model: platform takes a percentage of marketplace transactions

7.3 Phase 3: Intelligence Layer
- AI-powered engagement recommendations for CU admins
- Member sentiment analysis from community interactions
- Predictive analytics: identify members at risk of leaving
- Benchmarking across the GRYD network (anonymized, aggregated)

7.4 Phase 4: Platform Expansion
- Open API for CU-specific integrations
- White-label SDK for embedding GRYD features into existing CU apps
- Cross-CU features (with consent): shared events, vendor networks
- International expansion to credit union markets outside the US

7.5 The Long-Term Vision
- GRYD becomes the operating system for credit union member relationships
- Every interaction between a CU and its members flows through the platform
- The data layer becomes the most valuable asset: understanding what drives member loyalty across thousands of institutions
- Exit potential: strategic acquisition by a core banking provider, CU technology company, or financial services platform
